About Patrick Lehner

International perspective. Analytical discipline. Practical application.

Lehner Analytics is an independent, Berlin-based practice built on experience across financial analysis, political economy, data methods and technology research.

International background

From political economy to financial and technology analysis.

Patrick Lehner has studied and worked across Germany, the United Kingdom and China. This cross-disciplinary path supports a broad but structured view of markets, institutions, technology and financial outcomes.

Based in Berlin, Germany patrick-lehner@gmx.de Selected analytical projects
Munich

Philosophy, Chinese Studies and data methods

Studies at LMU Munich developed foundations in formal logic, philosophy of science, economics, statistics and programming. A semester in China added direct international and language experience.

London

Political economy and investment research

An MSc in Political Economy of Emerging Markets at King’s College London was followed by financial analysis covering capital-market fragility, digital assets and distributed-ledger technologies.

Munich

Cybersecurity and digital identity

At secunet Security Networks, research focused on digital identity systems, market developments and technical protocols, including the European Digital Identity Wallet.

Berlin

Financial modelling and tokenised assets

Current work includes bottom-up financial and cash-flow models, market and investment research, scenario analysis, monitoring frameworks and investor-facing analytical materials for tokenised real-world assets.

How this translates into client work: political economy provides context for markets and regulation; financial modelling tests the economics; data methods support transparent analysis; and technology research helps assess non-standard or fast-evolving business models. Together, these capabilities support scenario analysis, stress testing, due diligence and recurring executive intelligence.

Working principle

Rigour matters most when the answer is not obvious.

The aim is to make assumptions explicit, uncertainty manageable and implications useful for the decision at hand.
